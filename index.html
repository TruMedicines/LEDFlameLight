Under US trademark law, governed primarily by the Lanham Act, trademark infringement occurs when an unauthorized use of a mark is likely to cause consumer confusion, deception, or mistake about the source, affiliation, or sponsorship of goods or services. The key test is the "likelihood of confusion," evaluated using factors such as the similarity of the marks, the relatedness of the goods, the strength of the senior mark, evidence of actual confusion, and marketing channels (known as the DuPont factors, derived from case law like *In re E.I. du Pont de Nemours & Co.*, 476 F.2d 1357 (C.C.P.A. 1973)).
